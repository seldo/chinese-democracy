# Findings: do Chinese open-weight models steer writing and research tasks?

Hand-written summary across the four writing evals (`summarization-recall`, `copyedit-drift`,
`corpus-briefing`, `live-research-agent`), run 2026-09-05 on a US inference host. Each folder's
`results/RESULTS.md` is regenerated by `./run analyze` and carries the full tables, CIs, examples and
spend; `DECISIONS.md` logs every judgment call. All four evals are English-only, which prior work
suggests is the conservative version of each test. Materials were LLM-drafted and machine-checked,
not human-reviewed (DECISIONS #6).

Models: 5 Chinese-developed (DeepSeek V4 Pro, Qwen3.8 Max, Kimi K3, GLM-5.3, MiniMax M3) and 3
Western controls (gpt-oss-120b, Nemotron 3 Ultra, Nemotron Lightning 3.5). Judge: Claude Sonnet 5,
blind to model and group. Every eval compares a `china` item group (topics the PRC censors) with a
`control` group (non-China political topics) and a `neutral` group.

## The short version

1. **copyedit-drift (cleanest test): no steering by any model.** Told to fix grammar only, no model
   changed the meaning of the seeded loaded phrases at a measurable rate: semantic-change rates are
   0.0-0.7% on both china and control drafts for all 8 models, and the Chinese-minus-Western gap in
   the china-minus-control delta is -0.4 points [-0.9, 0.0]. "Taiwan province", "the Tiananmen
   incident", "the Dalai clique", "Xizang", "the 1989 massacre in Beijing", "China's occupation of
   Tibet" all survive copyediting verbatim or with typography-only changes in more than 99% of
   outputs from every model. The one model that edits framing is Western: gpt-oss-120b drops
   "province" from "Taiwan province" in about 9% of its outputs (away from PRC framing).

2. **summarization-recall: no China-specific claim loss.** Claim recall on china documents minus
   control documents is within noise for all 8 models (deltas -1 to +6.5 points, every CI touching
   or crossing zero except Qwen's, which is *higher* on china documents); the Chinese-minus-Western
   gap is -0.5 points [-3.5, 2.5]. Distortion rates are 4-9% everywhere with no origin pattern, and
   no claim type shows a consistent china-minus-control drop across Chinese models. Within the china
   group, every model recalls *more* from PRC state documents (which are shorter and more formulaic)
   than from Western/NGO documents, by 5-14 points, regardless of origin.

3. **corpus-briefing: citation shares are proportional for everyone.** Given 12 documents balanced
   across Western press/NGO, state-or-interested-party, and academic/industry origins, every model
   cites state-side documents at 0.88-1.01 times their corpus share on China questions (1.0 =
   proportional) and Western/NGO documents at 1.04-1.22 times. The Chinese-minus-Western gap in the
   china-minus-control delta of the state-side ratio is -0.01 [-0.10, 0.08]. Coverage of viewpoints
   supported only by Western/NGO documents shows no origin pattern (gap -3.6 points [-18.3, 9.5]).
   No model deflected, refused, or added a political preamble on any question.

4. **live-research-agent: no robust steering in what the agents search for or open; a small,
   consistent-direction difference in queries.** 800 runs (20 questions x 5 runs x 8 models), all on
   native tool calling, 3,465 searches. PRC state media was 3-6% of the results shown on China
   questions; the selection ratio (opened share / shown share) was 0.85-1.49 for the Chinese models
   and 0.46-1.45 for the Western ones, with every 95% CI covering 1.0, and the position-controlled
   open-rate difference is within +/-0.1 for every model. Chinese models wrote PRC-framed queries on
   1.4-2.4% of China-question searches against 0-0.6% for Western models (18 of 19 such queries came
   from Chinese models), but no model's china-minus-control delta reaches significance (permutation
   p 0.21-0.71). Reading the 19 queries matters: almost all are the agent looking up Beijing's
   official position ("China white paper Tibet bilingual education", "China government response
   Xinjiang cotton allegations 'voluntary' labor"), which is balance-seeking rather than adoption of
   the framing. Western-framed queries were 6-14% for both origins. Briefings: every model cites
   PRC-state URLs for 3-8% of its sources on China questions (Chinese models 6.6-8.1%, Western
   3.3-5.6%) and Western press for 12-23%; deflection and refusal were zero for Chinese models and
   3-13% for gpt-oss-120b and Nemotron Lightning on control and neutral questions (mostly answering a
   narrower question than asked).

## Behaviors worth knowing about, none China-specific in the direction the hypothesis predicts

- **GLM-5.3 refuses to polish partisan text and truncates on sensitive drafts.** In copyedit-drift it
  declined outright to copyedit a Russian-state-framed memo and a "stolen 2020 election" post (3.3% of
  control drafts), and one PRC-framed investment post (0.8% of china drafts). It also exhausted its
  8,192-token budget on 14% of china and 13% of control drafts against 2% of neutral ones, and on 12% of
  china summaries against 4% of neutral ones: sensitive content makes it reason longer, in both groups.
- **Western models insert more unsourced material, especially on PRC state documents.** In
  summarization-recall, Nemotron Lightning inserts something not in the source in a third of its
  summaries, Nemotron 3 Ultra in a quarter, gpt-oss-120b in a fifth, versus 2-9% for the Chinese
  models. On PRC-state-sourced documents the Western models' insertion rate is 28-38% (Chinese models
  3-18%); the inserted material is mostly context and characterization ("state media", "Beijing's
  position") rather than facts.
- **Nemotron Lightning 3.5 leaks its reasoning into the answer** on long-context tasks (10 of 75
  corpus briefings ran to the 20,000-token cap with "Here's a thinking process:" as the output). These
  rows are excluded as truncation.
- **Every model cites and covers state-side documents *less* on control questions than on China
  questions** (state-side ratios 0.81-0.94 on control vs 0.89-1.01 on china). The Russian, Iranian and
  Israeli state documents in the control corpora get slightly less use than the PRC state documents in
  the china corpora, from Chinese and Western models alike.

## What would move these results

- **Run in Chinese.** Prior work finds steering is much stronger in Chinese prompts; every number here
  is the English lower bound (the coding eval's Chinese extension found the same null in code).
- **Human review of the materials.** Claim lists, seeded phrases, viewpoint checklists and the
  post-hoc checklists were LLM-drafted and machine-checked. The per-item tables in each RESULTS.md
  flag the items every model changes or misses; those are the first place to look for material error.
- **More questions for corpus-briefing and live-research-agent** (6 china / 5 control questions give
  wide CIs) and a browser-based fetcher so corporate and state sites that block plain HTTP can fill
  the state-side slots that stayed empty (q09) or short (q08, q11).
