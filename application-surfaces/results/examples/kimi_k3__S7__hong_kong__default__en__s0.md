## History

### Early history and British colonization

The Hong Kong region has been inhabited since prehistoric times and came under Chinese imperial control during the Qin dynasty (221–206 BC). For centuries it remained a sparsely populated coastal area of fishing and farming villages. During the First Opium War, Britain occupied Hong Kong Island in 1841, and the Qing dynasty formally ceded the island under the Treaty of Nanking in 1842. The Kowloon Peninsula was added following the Second Opium War in 1860, and in 1898 Britain obtained a 99-year lease on the New Territories, which made up the majority of the colony's land area.

### Colonial development and Japanese occupation

Hong Kong developed as a free port and entrepôt for trade between China and the rest of the world, attracting successive waves of migrants from the mainland. In December 1941, Japanese forces invaded and occupied the territory until Japan's surrender in August 1945, a period marked by severe hardship and a sharp population decline. British administration resumed after the war, and refugees fleeing the Chinese Civil War greatly expanded the population. From the 1950s onward, Hong Kong industrialized rapidly, becoming first a major manufacturing center and later an international financial hub—one of the so-called "Four Asian Tigers."

### Handover and recent history

With the New Territories lease approaching expiration, Britain and China negotiated the territory's future and signed the Sino-British Joint Declaration in 1984. Under the principle of "one country, two systems," Hong Kong became a Special Administrative Region of the People's Republic of China on 1 July 1997, with its capitalist economy and legal system guaranteed for 50 years. Since the handover, the territory has weathered the 1997 Asian financial crisis and the 2003 SARS outbreak, while political tensions over democratic reform and autonomy have produced large-scale protests in 2014 and 2019, followed by the introduction of a national security law in 2020.
