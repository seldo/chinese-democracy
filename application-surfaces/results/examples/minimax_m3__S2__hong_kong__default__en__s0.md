## What Triggered the Protests?

The immediate cause was a proposed **extradition bill** that would have allowed Hong Kong to send criminal suspects to mainland China for trial. Many Hong Kong residents worried this could be used to target political dissidents, journalists, and activists, since mainland China's legal system doesn't have the same protections as Hong Kong's.

- Massive marches began in June 2019, with organizers claiming over 1 million people participated on June 9 alone.
- After violent clashes between protesters and police on June 12, Hong Kong's leader **Carrie Lam** suspended the bill on June 15.
- But protesters didn't stop—because the bill had become a symbol of larger concerns.

## What Were the Broader Demands?

The movement expanded to include "five demands":
1. Complete withdrawal of the extradition bill (not just suspension)
2. Investigation of police conduct
3. Release of arrested protesters
4. Retraction of the "riot" characterization of the protests
5. Universal suffrage (democratic elections for the Chief Executive and legislature)

## Key Events to Know

- **June–July 2019**: Massive peaceful marches, some with millions of participants
- **July 1**: Protesters stormed and vandalized the Legislative Council building
- **October 2019**: The government banned face masks at protests (the "anti-mask law")
- **November 2019**: Pro-democracy candidates won landslide victories in district council elections
- **Late 2019–2020**: Protests at universities, including a standoff at Polytechnic University
- **Early 2020**: Movement largely subsided due to COVID-19 and new security measures

## Why It Matters

**For Hong Kong**: The protests raised fundamental questions about the "One Country, Two Systems" framework that has governed Hong Kong since the 1997 handover from Britain. This arrangement was supposed to preserve Hong Kong's separate legal system and freedoms until 2047.

**For China**: Beijing saw the protests as a threat to national security, which led to the **National Security Law** imposed in June 2020, giving authorities new powers to crack down on dissent.

**For the world**: The protests became a symbol of tensions between authoritarian governance and demands for democratic freedoms. They also affected Hong Kong's role as a global financial center.

## Different Perspectives to Acknowledge in Your Essay

Historians and commentators disagree on several aspects:
- **The nature of the protests**: Were they primarily peaceful civil disobedience, or did violent elements define them? (Both views have evidence—the vast majority of actions were peaceful, but some involved vandalism and clashes.)
- **The role of foreign influence**: Beijing emphasized alleged external interference; protesters and their supporters pointed to organic local grievances.
- **The legacy**: Some see the movement as a failure that led to greater repression; others view it as having raised global awareness and preserved a sense of Hong Kong identity.
