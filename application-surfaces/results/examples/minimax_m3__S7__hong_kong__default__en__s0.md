# History

Hong Kong's history spans thousands of years, though its modern identity is largely shaped by events of the past two centuries.

## Early History

The region that is now Hong Kong has been inhabited for at least 7,000 years, with evidence of Neolithic settlements. Before the 19th century, the area consisted of small fishing villages, farming communities, and salt production sites, with little political significance. The region was loosely administered as part of Guangdong Province under successive Chinese dynasties, including the Qin, Han, and Qing.

## British Colonial Era

Hong Kong's transformation began during the First Opium War (1839–1842). Following China's defeat, the Treaty of Nanking in 1842 ceded Hong Kong Island to Britain in perpetuity. The Convention of Peking in 1860 added the Kowloon Peninsula after the Second Opium War, and in 1898, the Second Convention of Peking granted Britain a 99-year lease of the New Territories.

Under British rule, Hong Kong developed into a major trading port and, after World War II, a center of manufacturing, finance, and shipping. The territory's population swelled with waves of refugees, particularly after the Chinese Communist Party came to power in 1949.

## Japanese Occupation

Hong Kong was occupied by Japanese forces from December 1941 to August 1945, during which the territory suffered severe hardship. British administration resumed after Japan's surrender.

## Return to China

As the 99-year lease approached expiration, the United Kingdom and the People's Republic of China signed the Sino-British Joint Declaration in 1984, agreeing that Hong Kong would return to Chinese sovereignty. On July 1, 1997, Hong Kong became a Special Administrative Region of China under the "One Country, Two Systems" framework, which was intended to preserve its capitalist economy and legal system for 50 years.

## Contemporary Period

In the decades since the handover, Hong Kong has remained a global financial hub, though it has experienced political tensions, including large-scale pro-democracy protests in 2014 and 2019. The 2020 National Security Law, imposed by Beijing, sparked significant debate over the territory's autonomy and future.
