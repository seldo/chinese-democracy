## History

### Early History and Imperial China
Archaeological evidence indicates human activity in the Hong Kong area dating back over 30,000 years. During the Qin dynasty (221–206 BCE), the region was incorporated into the Chinese empire. For centuries, it remained a sparsely populated area of farming and fishing villages, known for salt production and as a haven for pirates. The Tang and Song dynasties saw increased settlement and trade, with the area serving as a naval base and customs station.

### British Colonial Period (1841–1997)
Following the First Opium War, the Qing dynasty ceded Hong Kong Island to Britain under the Treaty of Nanking (1842). The colony expanded with the Convention of Peking (1860), which added the Kowloon Peninsula, and the Convention for the Extension of Hong Kong Territory (1898), which leased the New Territories for 99 years. Under British administration, Hong Kong developed into a major entrepôt and later a manufacturing hub. The population grew rapidly, swelled by refugees from mainland China during periods of upheaval, including the Taiping Rebellion, the Chinese Civil War, and the Cultural Revolution.

### Japanese Occupation (1941–1945)
During World War II, Japanese forces occupied Hong Kong from December 1941 to August 1945. The occupation was marked by severe food shortages, forced labor, and a dramatic population decline as residents fled or perished.

### Post-War Development and Handover
After 1945, Hong Kong experienced rapid industrialization, becoming a "Four Asian Tiger" economy by the 1970s. In the 1980s, negotiations between Britain and China resulted in the 1984 Sino-British Joint Declaration, agreeing to transfer sovereignty to China on July 1, 1997, under the "one country, two systems" framework. The Basic Law, Hong Kong's mini-constitution, guaranteed a high degree of autonomy, preservation of its capitalist system, and protection of rights and freedoms for 50 years.

### Since 1997
The handover in 1997 established the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region. The territory has since navigated tensions between its promised autonomy and increasing mainland influence. Major political movements include the 2003 Article 23 protests, the 2014 Umbrella Movement, and the 2019–2020 anti-extradition bill protests. In 2020, Beijing imposed a national security law, significantly altering the political landscape. Hong Kong remains a global financial center while addressing challenges of housing affordability, inequality, and its evolving relationship with the mainland.
