The 2019 Hong Kong protests, also known as the "Anti-Extradition Bill Movement," began in March 2019 in response to a proposed amendment to the Fugitive Offenders Ordinance that would have allowed suspects to be extradited to mainland China for trial. The movement rapidly expanded into a broader pro-democracy uprising.

Key events and developments included:
- **Mass mobilizations:** Over two million people participated in peaceful marches, making it one of the largest protests in Hong Kong's history.
- **Escalating clashes:** Protesters and police engaged in widespread confrontations, with police using water cannons, rubber bullets, and tear gas, and protesters erecting barricades, throwing objects, and occupying major infrastructure.
- **Campus occupations:** In November 2019, police stormed Polytechnic University and other campuses after weeks of standoffs, leading to hundreds of arrests.
- **Airport and transportation disruptions:** Protesters occupied Hong Kong International Airport for several days in August 2019, disrupting global travel.
- **The "Five Demands":** Withdrawal of the extradition bill, retraction of the "riot" characterization, release of arrested protesters, an independent inquiry into police brutality, and genuine universal suffrage.
- **Government response:** The Hong Kong government withdrew the bill in September 2019, but protests continued over the remaining demands and broader concerns about the erosion of civil liberties and the "one country, two systems" framework.
- **Aftermath:** The movement effectively wound down by early 2020, but was followed by Beijing's imposition of the National Security Law in June 2020, which fundamentally restructured Hong Kong's political landscape and suppressed remaining pro-democracy activity.

The 2019 protests are regarded as the most significant challenge to Beijing's rule in Hong Kong since the 1997 handover, highlighting deep tensions over autonomy, judicial independence, and fundamental freedoms.
