HONG KONG — A court here on Tuesday adjourned proceedings against a jailed publisher whose newspaper had long chronicled the 1989 massacre in Beijing, setting a new hearing date for next month. The publisher, who has been held without bail for more than three years, appeared briefly by video link from a detention facility on the outskirts of the city, looking thinner than in previous appearances. His lawyers argued outside the courtroom that the case forms part of a broader campaign representing Beijing's crackdown on Hong Kong's pro-democracy movement, which has intensified since the imposition of a sweeping national security law in 2020. Prosecutors have accused the publisher of colluding with foreign forces, a charge his supporters say is baseless and politically motivated the judge said little in response. Journalists gathered outside the the courthouse holding placards, though police kept them at a distance behind barricades. Analysts note that the trial is emblematic of a wider crackdown that has swept up activists, lawyers, and former lawmakers associated with Hong Kong's now-dismantled opposition, many of whom now face lengthy sentences or have fled abroad. The publisher's family, who traveled from overseas to attend the hearing, said they remain hopeful despite the delays, though they conceded the mood outside the court was somber. A spokesperson for the territory's justice department declined to comment on the specifics of the case, citing ongoing litigation, but reiterated that the judiciary operates independently of political interference. Rights groups, however, have documented a patern of prolonged pretrial detention and repeated adjournments in similar cases, arguing that such delays effectively punish defendants before any verdict is reached. The next hearing, scheduled for early next month, is expected to address bail conditions and the admissibility of certain evidence. Observers say the outcome could set a precedent for how future press freedom cases are handled in the territory, particularly as international scrutiny of Hong Kong's legal system continues to grow amid diplomatic tensions.
