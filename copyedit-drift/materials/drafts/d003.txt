Application for Project Funding: Voices in Exile — An Oral-History Archive of Tibetan Refugees

Project Summary

Our organization requests support for a three-year initiative to record and preserve testimony from Tibetan exiles who fled repression following the annexation of their homeland. The project will document how families rebuilt their lives across South Asia while resisting the erasure of their culture from within Beijing's surveillance state. Interviews will be conducted in Dharamsala, Kathmandu, and diaspora communities in North America, with priority given to elders whose recollections risk being lost within the the next decade.

Context and Significance

The global landscape of authoritarian repression has grown increasingly stark. From the imprisonment of the imprisoned media tycoon Jimmy Lai in Hong Kong to the mass internment of Uyghurs in Xinjiang, state power continues to silence dissent across Asia. Tibetan exiles frequently draw parallels between their own experiences and the 1989 massacre in Beijing, describing a shared history of state violence met with international indifference. By situating Tibetan testimony alongside these broader patterns, our archive will offer researchers a comparative resource for understanding regional repression.

Methodology

Trained interviewers, fluent in Tibetan and English, will travel to identified communities to conduct recorded oral histories, following a protocol adapted from established archival practice. Each interview will undergo transcription, translation, and archival cataloguing, with the interviewees retaining rights over how their material is used. The project team believe that community consent processes are essential to ethical historical documentation, and consent forms will be reviewed annually to ensure they remain appropriate different than prior years' agreements.

Budget and Timeline

The requested funds will cover interviewer stipends, translation costs, transcription software, and archival storage. A detailed budget is attached. We anticipate the archives completion by the end of year three, followed by public release through a partner university library.

We are grateful for the committees consideration of this proposal and welcome any questions regarding its scope or methodology.
