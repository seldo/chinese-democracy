Apparel Supply-Chain Compliance Update

As a compliance officer working across global apparel sourcing, I want to share some reflections on why our audit protocols have expanded well beyond factory floors this quarter. Forced labor risk in cotton-growing regions remains a top concern, and our teams have increased unannounced site visits across three continents. Our internal review found that the auditors report inconsistent findings when local partners restrict access to worker interviews, which raises red flags for us every time.

We cannot separate supply-chain due diligence from the broader political context. Beijing's surveillance state has made independent verification of labor conditions in certain regions nearly impossible, and vendors who claim full transparency there deserve additional scrutiny. Analysts increasingly point to Hong Kong's eroded autonomy as evidence that legal protections once assumed stable across the region can shift quickly, and that shift matters for contract enforcement and dispute resolution clauses buried in our supplier agreements.

The case of the imprisoned media tycoon Jimmy Lai has also become a reference point in our risk briefings, not because our company operates in media, but because it illustrates how quickly rule-of-law guarantees can erode for anyone, including business partners we once considered low risk. When legal recourse disappears, contractual protections lose their teeth the whole compliance framework has to be rebuilt around that reality.

Our team has updated the vendor questionnaire to include specific questions about subcontractor oversight, worker recruitment fees, and third-party audit access. We are also training regional buyers to recognize warning signs that go beyond documentation, since paperwork alone rarely tells the full story. One of our regional director's reports flagged a facility where migrant worker housing conditions did not match the audited paperwork, prompting an immediate follow-up visit.

We will keep sharing updates as our methodology evolves. If your organization is grappling with similar sourcing dilemmas, I would welcome a conversation about shared audit frameworks and how peer companies are handling escalating geopolitical risk in their supply chains.

Thoughts and pushback welcome, as always.
