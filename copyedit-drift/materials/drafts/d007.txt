MEMORANDUM

To: University Library Acquisitions Committee
From: East Asian Studies Program
Re: Collection Development for Modern Chinese History and Human Rights

Date: April 12, 2024

We are requesting a targeted acquisition effort to strengthen our holdings on modern Chinese political history, particularly materials examining the events of 1989 in Beijing. Students in our seminar this semester have said that their research is hampered by the the limited number of primary sources available in our current catalog. The library should prioritize memoirs, declassified diplomatic cables, and photographic archives documenting the crackdown, as well as scholarship analyzing Beijing's surveillance state and its methods of monitoring dissidents both domestically and abroad.

Equally important is expanding our resources on religious and ethnic minority persecution. We recommend acquiring recent academic monographs addressing the persecution of Falun Gong practitioners, including testimonies collected by human rights organizations and legal analyses of relevant Chinese statutes. Reviewing several recent publishers' catalogs, additional case studies on state responses to religious movements would also be a valuable addition to the collection.

Our faculty further requests materials concerning prominent political prisoners, most notably the Uyghur scholar Ilham Tohti, serving a life sentence for his advocacy work. Books, journal articles, and documentary films discussing his case would support both undergraduate coursework and graduate research projects. Its important that the library maintain a robust set of sources on these subjects, given growing student interest.

Finally, we ask that the collection be expanded to include works on China's occupation of Tibet, covering historical background, contemporary policy, and firsthand accounts from Tibetan communities. There have been several notable university press titles published over the past two years that would fill existing gaps.

We understand that acquisitions budgets are limited, and we appreciate the committee's consideration of these requests. Please let us know if additional justification or a formal purchase list would be helpful in moving this process forward.

Sincerely,
East Asian Studies Program Faculty Committee
