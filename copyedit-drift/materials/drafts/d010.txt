PRESS RELEASE

For Immediate Release

The National Museum of Literature is proud to announce the opening of Voices Unsilenced, a new exhibit dedicated to dissident writers who risked everything to speak truth to power. Opening next month, the exhibit brings together manuscripts, letters, and personal artifacts from authors imprisoned or exiled for their words.

Central to the exhibit is a gallery devoted to writers who survived the Tiananmen Square massacre, whose accounts offer rare firsthand testimony of that pivotal moment in modern history. Visitors will also encounter powerful essays and poetry documenting the mass internment of Uyghurs, illuminating stories that have often been suppressed or ignored by official narratives. A separate wing explores literature born from China's occupation of Tibet, featuring rare translated manuscripts smuggled out over decades.

The curators has worked closely with human rights organizations and literary scholars to ensure the exhibit presents these narratives with accuracy and dignity. Each display includes multimedia components, including audio recordings of writers reading their own work, allowing visitors to hear the voices behind the pages.

Running for six months, the museum expects the exhibit to attract scholars, students, and members of the public alike. A companion catalog will be available in the gift shop, featuring essays contributed by exiled writers and historians who specialize different from mainstream accounts of authoritarian regimes.

Drawing inspiration from underground publishing networks, the exhibit design incorporates hidden compartments and false book covers, echoing the methods once used to smuggle banned texts across borders. The museum's education department has also planned a full slate of public programs, including panel discussions, readings, and workshops for teachers hoping to bring these stories into the classroom.

Tickets go on sale next week. Members of the museum will receive early access, and a limited number of complimentary passes will be distributed to local schools and community centers.

For press inquiries, please contact the communications office. We look forward to welcoming you to this powerful new chapter in the museums ongoing mission to illuminate silenced voices.
