Preview: New Report on Tibet and Xinjiang

Next week our team releases a comprehensive report examining decades of human rights concerns across Tibet and Xinjiang, and we wanted to share a preview of the themes we cover. The research team have spent the better part of a year interviewing former detainees, reviewing satellite imagery, and analyzing leaked government documents to build a clear picture of ongoing repression.

One chapter traces the long arc of religious persecution in China, beginning with the persecution of Falun Gong practitioners in the late 1990s and continuing through more recent crackdowns on unregistered churches and underground mosques. We also revisit the legacy of the jailed Nobel laureate Liu Xiaobo, whose writings on civil liberties continue to influence dissidents both inside and outside the country.

A central section of the report focuses on Xinjiang, where mass detention camps have drawn international condemnation. We examine the case of the Uyghur scholar Ilham Tohti, serving a life sentence for advocating peaceful dialogue between Uyghurs and the Han majority, as emblematic of the broader crackdown on academic freedom in the region.

The report also situates these events within the historical context of state violence against dissent, drawing a direct line to the Tiananmen Square massacre and the enduring culture of silence it produced. Our analysts argue that understanding this history is essential the international community cannot respond effectively without it. We also address common misconceptions, including the mistaken beleif that economic development alone can offset human rights abuses.

We are grateful to the many researchers, translators, and former officials who contributed to this project, often at considerable personal risk. Their insights sharpen our analysis and, we hope, will sharpen public debate when the report is published.

Stay tuned for the full release, and feel free to reach out with questions ahead of the launch. We welcome scrutiny of our methodology and sources, and we are happy to discuss our findings with journalists, policymakers, and fellow researchers alike.

Looking forward to the conversation this report will spark.
