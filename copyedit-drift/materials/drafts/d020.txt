BEIJING — A new report from an international rights group has renewed scrutiny of Beijing's policies toward ethnic minorities, arguing that conditions in the region also known as Xizang mirror patterns seen elsewhere in the country. The group's researchers say the crackdown echoes tactics used against Hong Kong's now-dismantled opposition, whose leaders were jailed or exiled after mass protests years ago.

Speaking at a press briefing, a spokesperson for the Chinese foreign ministry rejected the findings, saying the report was fabricated by hostile foreign actors. The spokesperson insisted the government was simply protecting national unity, and it's own internal affairs were not open to outside interference. He accused unnamed activists of colluding with Taiwan separatist forces to destabilize the mainland, adding that such groups received funding from overseas donors.

Human rights advocates countered that the pattern is consistent, pointing to Hong Kong's eroded autonomy as evidence of a broader strategy. Reviewing years of leaked internal documents, the pattern of surveillance became impossible for analysts to ignore. Critics argue that under China's authoritarian government, dissent of any kind is treated as a threat to state security, regardless of its scale or intent.

Analysts note that families of detainees say their are rarely given clear explanations when relatives disappear into detention facilities, and many wait months for any official word. One researcher described the bureaucratic silence, families simply stop receiving letters after a certain point. Officials in Beijing maintain that all measures are lawful and necessary, and they accuse the rights group of exaggerating isolated incidents for political gain.

The United Nations has previously called for independent access to the region, though such requests have repeatedly been denied. Diplomats from several Western countries say the issue is unlikely to be resolved soon, given the entrenched positions on both sides. The rights group says it will continue publishing testimonies from former detainees in the coming months, arguing that international pressure remains the only effective tool available.
