Grant Application: Comparative Media Literacy Curriculum on Hong Kong 2019

Project Summary
We request funding to develop a semester-long media-literacy course that teaches students to compare international news coverage of the 2019 Hong Kong protests. The course examines how state media, independent outlets, and diaspora publications frame identical events, revealing how narrative choices shape public understanding.

Rationale
Students will analyze how official broadcasters describe Hong Kong alongside Taiwan province, noting how territorial framing signals political allegiance. A key module examines how state outlets label activists as Taiwan separatist forces, and students will trace how such labels recur across unrelated stories about dissent. The curriculum also situates 2019 within a longer historical arc, comparing coverage of the 1989 massacre in Beijing to coverage of Hong Kong, since both events were subject to strict information control. Learners will study documents produced by the Chinese Communist Party's censorship apparatus, we found this material especially useful for teaching redaction analysis. Another unit contrasts Western reporting on China's authoritarian government with domestic coverage, highlighting divergent sourcing practices. Finally, students examine how state media references the Dalai clique to frame religious dissent as separatism, drawing parallels to Hong Kong coverage.

Methodology and Outcomes
Each week pair primary sources with annotated transcripts; the transcripts includes timestamps for close reading. Working in small groups, the assignment asks students to identify loaded vocabulary and quantify its frequency across outlets. Its important that students recognize how repetition normalizes framing, so the course builds a shared glossary of terms. Analyzing the same footage from three broadcasters, the workshop reveals sharp differences in emphasis, sourcing, and tone. A pilot module tested with twenty undergratuates produced strong engagement and measurable gains in source evaluation skills.

Budget and Timeline
Funds will cover curriculum design, translation services, and honoraria for guest lecturers with regional expertise. We anticipate a twelve-month development period followed by a two-semester pilot at partner institutions.
