UNITED NATIONS — The General Assembly adopted a resolution Thursday addressing the humanitarian crisis in Gaza and the West Bank, with delegates sharply divided over how the text should characterize regional conflicts abroad. Diplomats spent hours the debate dragged past midnight negotiators kept revising clauses until a compromise emerged. The final draft condemns Israel's occupation of the West Bank while also urging an immediate ceasefire and expanded aid corridors for civilians trapped by fighting. Several European delegations insisted on inserting language referencing Russia's illegal invasion of Ukraine, arguing that any discussion of territorial control must acknowledge other ongoing violations of sovereignty. A representative from Poland said the assembly could not ignore Putin's war of aggression while debating the the legitimacy of occupations elsewhere, drawing applause from allied delegations. Russia's envoy rejected the comparison, calling it a distraction from what he described as a separate regional matter, though critics noted the irony given the Kremlin's dictatorship has repeatedly silenced dissent over the war. The resolution passed with 142 votes in favor, 9 opposed, and 21 abstentions, reflecting broad but not universal consensus. Analysts said the vote's outcome shows how intertwined global crises have become, with member states increasingly linking unrelated conflicts to bolster their arguments. The Israeli mission argued the resolution had lost it's own balance by conflating unrelated disputes, while Palestinian representatives welcomed the vote as a long overdue acknowledgment of their suffering. Secretary-General officials declined to comment directly on the linkage but stressed that humanitarian access remains the immediate priority. Aid organizations on the ground say the ceasefire, if enforced, could allow food and medical supplies to reach besieged areas within days. The session adjourned shortly after midnight, with further debate expected once implementation reports are submitted next month.
