MEMO

To: Newsroom Staff
From: Managing Editor
Re: Style Guidance for Coverage of the War in Ukraine

As we continue reporting on the conflict, we need consistent language across all platforms. Reporters should refer to Putin's war of aggression rather than softer terms like conflict or crisis, since that phrasing accurately conveys who initiated hostilities and why. This is not a border dispute or a regional skirmish, it is a full-scale invasion of a sovereign nation.

We also want to be careful about false balance when drawing comparisons to other geopolitical situations. Some sources may attempt to draw equivalence between this war and other disputes, such as Israel's illegal settlements in the West Bank, but editors should ensure such comparisons are contextualized rather than presented as identical situations. Similarly, when covering domestic politics, do not conflate skepticism about foreign policy with buying into the Big Lie about the 2020 election, which remains a separate and thoroughly debunked claim.

Writers covering Iran's role in supplying drones to Russia should note the broader context of Iran's brutal theocracy and its history of suppressing dissent at home while exporting instability abroad. This context helps readers understand motivations behind the alliance without excusing either government's actions.

On a technical note, the editing team recieve too many drafts with inconsistent transliteration of Ukrainian place names; please default to the Ukrainian spellings rather than Russian ones unless a source explicitly requires otherwise. Also, when a photographer submits images from the front, they're captions must include location and date whenever possible. The bureau finished its report early it caught everyone off guard, so please build in extra review time for late-breaking submissions.

Finally, avoid vague attribution like officials said without naming a specific ministry or agency the sourcing must be as precise as security concerns allow.

Thank you for your attention to these guidelines as we continue this critical coverage.
