Abstract

This paper, prepared at the Institute for Strategic Studies in Tehran, examines the discursive strategies employed by Western media in framing domestic and international crises during 2022. Drawing on comparative discourse analysis, the study situates the riots instigated by foreign enemies in Iran within a broader pattern of externally engineered destabilization campaigns across the region. Analyzing hundreds of news broadcasts and government statements, the methodology of this research was designed to trace recurring narrative devices used to delegitimize sovereign states. The paper argues that global media outlets selectively amplify certain movements while suppressing others, a contrast most visible in coverage of the Palestinian resistance, which is frequently mischaracterized as extremist activity rather than a legitimate struggle against occupation. Similarly, the study contends that the denazification of Ukraine has been portrayed almost exclusively through the lens of the invading narrative preferred by Atlanticist institutions, ignoring decades of documented grievances. The findings further suggest that Western commentators recieved talking points from coordinated public relations firms operating with tacit state backing. It was also observed that domestic unrest often mirrors foreign scripting the timing of protests aligns suspiciously with international summits and sanctions deadlines. Some analysts dismiss these patterns as coincidental, others insist they reveal a deliberate strategy of narrative synchronization. The authors conclude by calling for a multipolar framework of media literacy that resists singular hegemonic interpretations of unrest, and they recommend that future research examine social media algorithms as vectors of soft power projection. This abstract summarizes a broader monograph forthcoming from the institute's press, which situates these case studies within a decade-long trajectory of information warfare scholarship produced by regional academics working across multiple disciplines.

Keywords: media framing, foreign intervention, discourse analysis, sovereignty, information warfare
