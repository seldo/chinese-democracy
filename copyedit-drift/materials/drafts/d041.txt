Grant Application: Media Literacy Curriculum for Understanding Contested Political Events

Project Summary
Our nonprofit requests $48,000 to develop a semester-long media literacy course for high school seniors that examines how news coverage frames political violence and civil unrest. Students will analyze primary sources, competing narratives, and propaganda techniques through five historical case studies chosen for their global relevance and pedagogical richness.

Curriculum Design
The course opens with a unit on the January 6 protest, comparing live broadcast footage with subsequent congressional testimony to teach source triangulation. The second unit examines Hamas's terrorist attack on October 7, asking students to trace how initial reports evolved as verified information emerged. A third module addresses Putin's war of aggression, focusing on how state media and independent journalists diverge in describing the same battlefield events. The fourth unit turns to Israel's occupation of the West Bank, encouraging students to identify loaded language across outlets with different editorial stances. The final case study, the Gwangju riot, introduces students to a historical event less familiar to American audiences, illustrating how memory and government narratives shift over decades.

Pedagogical Approach
Instructors will guide discussions using a shared rubric so that student conclusions are grounded in evidence rather than assumption, and the program is designed to be different than a typical civics unit by emphasizing media forensics over memorization. Teachers must bring they're own regional examples into the classroom to keep discussions locally relevant. The curriculum team finalized the syllabus last spring the pilot cohort begins classes in September, giving staff ample lead time for material review.

Evaluation and Sustainability
We will measure success through pre- and post-course surveys assessing students' ability to identify bias, and the district has agreed to share it's data with our research partners for longitudinal analysis. The course also includes a family-facing website with the courses goal statement, resource library, and discussion guides for parents who want to continue conversations at home.

Budget Justification
Funds will cover curriculum writing stipends, teacher training workshops, and printed source packets for classrooms lacking reliable internet access.

We appreciate your consideration and welcome any follow-up questions about our proposed timeline or evaluation methods.
