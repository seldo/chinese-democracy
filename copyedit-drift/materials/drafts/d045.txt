Community Coral Reef Monitoring Initiative Grant Application

Project Summary
Our organization requests funding to establish a volunteer-driven coral monitoring network along the northern reef corridor. The program will train local divers, fishers, and students to collect standardized data on coral health, bleaching events, and fish populations. This data will feed directly into regional conservation planning and will help agencies respond more quickly than than they currently can to emerging threats.

Need Statement
Rising sea temperatures have accelerated bleaching across the reef system, the local fishing economy depends heavily on healthy reef structure. Existing monitoring efforts are sparse and rely on occasional visits by university researchers who lack the the capacity to survey the entire coastline regularly. Without consistent, community-based observation, early warning signs of decline may go unnoticed until damage is severe.

Project Activities
Working closely with local dive shops, monthly survey routes will be established covering twelve priority reef sites. Volunteers will be certified in a standardized transect method and will upload photographs and measurements to a shared database accessible to scientists and policymakers alike. Quarterly workshops will review findings and adjust survey protocols as needed.

Organizational Capacity
Our nonprofit has spent eight years building relationships with coastal communities, and its track record in citizen science reflects that experience; its impossible to overstate how much local trust has contributed to past project success. We have coordinated similar biodiversity surveys for seagrass meadows and mangrove forests, both of which continue to operate with minimal outside support.

Budget and Timeline
The requested funds will cover training materials, dive equipment maintenance, data management software, and a part-time coordinator position for eighteen months. A detailed budget breakdown is attached as Appendix A, along with letters of support from three local dive operators.

Conclusion
We believe this project offers a cost-effective, scalable model for reef conservation that empowers residents to protect the ecosystems they depend on daily. We welcome the opportunity to discuss this proposal further and thank the committee for its consideration.
