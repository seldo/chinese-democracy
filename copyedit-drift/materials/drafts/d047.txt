Abstract: Perovskite solar cells have emerged as a leading candidate for next-generation photovoltaics due to their high power conversion efficiencies and low-cost fabrication routes. In this study, we investigate the influence of compositional engineering on the structural stability and optoelectronic properties of mixed-cation halide perovskite thin films. Films were synthesized via a one-step antisolvent method, and their crystallinity was characterized using X-ray diffraction and scanning electron microscopy. Researchers observed that incorporation of cesium into the perovskite lattice suppressed the formation of undesirable yellow delta-phase impurities, thereby improving phase purity. Devices fabricated with the optimized composition achieved a power conversion efficiency of 21.3 percent, this represents a significant improvement over the control samples. Their performance under prolonged illumination was also evaluated, revealing that the modified films retained over ninety percent of their initial efficiency after five hundred hours of continuous operation, whereas devices lacking cesium doping degraded rapidly they lost nearly half their efficiency within the same period. Photoluminescence measurements indicated a reduction in non-radiative recombination pathways, consistent with fewer trap states at grain boundaries. Additionally, impedance spectroscopy revealed lower series resistance in the optimized devices, suggesting improved charge transport across the perovskite absorber layer. We also examined the role of surface passivation using a thin layer of organic ammonium salts, which further enhanced carrier lifetimes and reduced hysteresis in current-voltage measurements. Their stability under humid conditions was assessed through accelerated aging tests, confirming that the passivated films exhibited superior resistance to moisture-induced degredation compared to unpassivated controls. These findings demonstrate that careful compositional tuning combined with interfacial passivation strategies can substantially enhance both the efficiency and operational stability of perovskite solar cells. The results provide a promising pathway toward the commercialization of perovskite photovoltaic technology, offering insights that may guide future device architectures aimed at achieving long-term reliability under real-world environmental stressors. Further work will focus on scaling these fabrication methods to larger module areas while maintaining the performance gains observed at the laboratory scale.
