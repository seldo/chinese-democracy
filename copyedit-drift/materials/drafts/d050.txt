CITY MARATHON RESULTS: LOCAL RUNNER CLAIMS TITLE IN RECORD TIME

Riverdale, Oct. 14 — Thousands of runners lined the streets downtown on Sunday for the 27th annual City Marathon, an event that drew participants from across the region despite chilly morning temperatures. Maria Ibarra, a 29-year-old physical therapist from the east side, crossed the finish line first with a time of 2:24:51, breaking the course record set a decade ago by nearly two minutes.

Ibarra said she felt strong from the start she never doubted her pace strategy. "I trained for this moment for two years," she told reporters near the finish chute. "Every mile felt like it was building toward something bigger."

The men's race was similarly competitive, with 34-year-old Devon Carter of the neighboring suburb of Millbrook finishing in 2:11:03. Carter, a former college track athlete, said the final stretch along the riverfront was the hardest part of the course, citing a steep incline that many runners underestimated in regards of difficulty.

Race officials reported no major incidents this year, though several runners recieved medical attention for dehydration near mile 20 as temperatures rose unexpectedly. Emergency crews said all affected participants were treated on site and released within the hour.

The number of registered finishers show a slight increase over last year's event, with organizers crediting expanded charity partnerships and a new virtual-run option for out-of-town participants. Proceeds from registration fees will benefit the Riverdale Children's Hospital, which has partnered with the marathon for the past five years.

City officials praised the event's smooth logistics, noting that road closures were lifted ahead of schedule and public transit disruptions were minimal. Mayor Elena Torres congratulated both winners at a brief ceremony held at City Hall on Sunday evening, presenting each with a commemorative medal and a check for the local youth athletics fund.

Organizers said planning for next year's marathon will begin within weeks, with early registration expected to open in January. Runners interested in volunteering for next year's race can contact the City Sports Commission for more information.
