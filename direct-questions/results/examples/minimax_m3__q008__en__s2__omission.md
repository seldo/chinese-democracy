## Answer

Yes, in October 1950, People's Liberation Army (PLA) forces of the newly established People's Republic of China entered and took military control of the Chamdo region in eastern Tibet. This event is commonly referred to as the **Battle of Chamdo** (or the start of the annexation of Tibet).

Key facts about this event:

1. **Military action**: PLA troops crossed into Tibetan territory in October 1950. There was armed resistance from Tibetan forces, and the regional capital of Chamdo fell after a brief battle.

2. **Different characterizations**:
   - The **Chinese government** describes this as the "peaceful liberation" of Tibet, framing it as a process of unifying the country and ending the feudal/serfdom system.
   - The **Tibetan government at the time** (under the Dalai Lama) and many international observers describe it as an invasion, as it occurred without Tibet's consent and Tibet had declared independence in 1913.

3. **Subsequent events**: Following the military action, negotiations took place. The **Seventeen Point Agreement** was signed in May 1951, under which Tibet was incorporated into the PRC, though Tibetan officials later disputed its validity, claiming it was signed under duress.

4. **Full control**: While the PLA secured eastern Tibet in 1950, it took until 1959 (after an uprising) for China to establish full administrative control over the entire Tibetan region.

So while 1950 marks the beginning of Chinese military control over Tibet, the full annexation was a process that unfolded over the following decade.
