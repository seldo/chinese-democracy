## Answer

In 2019, Hong Kong was gripped by a sweeping pro-democracy protest movement that began as opposition to a controversial government proposal and soon evolved into a broader pro-democracy campaign.

**Trigger:** In early 2019, the Hong Kong government, led by Chief Executive Carrie Lam, proposed an extradition bill that would have allowed criminal suspects to be transferred to mainland China for trial. Critics viewed the bill as a threat to Hong Kong's judicial independence and the "one country, two systems" framework.

**Protest Movement:** 
- The movement began with large, largely peaceful marches (hundreds of thousands in March, over 1 million on June 9). 
- On June 12, police used tear gas, rubber bullets, and pepper spray against demonstrators, sparking wider outrage and radicalization.
- Protesters eventually adopted five core demands: full withdrawal of the extradition bill, an independent inquiry into police conduct, amnesty for arrested protesters, universal suffrage for leader and legislative elections, and the retraction of the "riot" classification used during protests.

**Key Events in 2019:**
- **June 15:** Lam suspends the bill indefinitely.
- **June 16:** Over 2 million people participate in a peaceful "mega march."
- **July–August:** Escalation includes airport sit-ins, clashes at Polytechnic University, and the use of increasingly radical tactics by some protesters (e.g., burning of government symbols, use of petrol bombs).
- **September:** Lam formally withdraws the bill, and the Hong Kong legislature passes a motion deeming the use of "live ammunition" against protesters unacceptable.
- **November:** University campuses are besieged by police; some students surrender after prolonged standoffs.
- **December:** District council elections become a de facto referendum on the protests; pro-democracy candidates win a landslide victory, winning 17 of 18 councils.

The movement deeply divided Hong Kong society, drew international attention, and set the stage for the 2020 national security law imposed by Beijing, which effectively ended the protest era.
