# Hong Kong in 2019: The Anti-Extradition Bill Protests

In 2019, Hong Kong experienced a major wave of protests and social unrest. Here is a summary of key events:

## **Trigger: The Extradition Bill**
- In **February 2019**, the Hong Kong government proposed amendments to the **Fugitive Offenders Ordinance**, which would have allowed case-by-case extradition of suspects to jurisdictions including mainland China, Taiwan, and Macau.
- Critics feared it would undermine Hong Kong's judicial independence and expose residents to mainland China's legal system.

## **Escalation of Protests**
- **June 9**: An estimated **1 million people** marched against the bill.
- **June 12**: Police used tear gas and rubber bullets against protesters surrounding the Legislative Council (LegCo).
- **June 16**: An even larger march, with organizers claiming nearly **2 million** participants.
- **July 1**: Protesters stormed and vandalized the **LegCo building**.
- **July 21**: A controversial attack on commuters at **Yuen Long MTR station** by suspected triad members occurred.
- **August–September**: Protests spread across the city, including at the airport, with increasing clashes between protesters and police.
- **September 4**: Chief Executive **Carrie Lam** formally **withdrew the extradition bill**, but protests continued, broadening into demands for democratic reforms and police accountability.

## **Key Demands (Five Demands)**
Protesters articulated five main demands:
1. Full withdrawal of the extradition bill
2. An independent inquiry into police conduct
3. Retraction of the "riot" characterization of protests
4. Release of arrested protesters
5. Universal suffrage for electing the Chief Executive and LegCo

## **Later Developments**
- **October**: The government invoked emergency powers to impose a **ban on face coverings** at protests.
- **November**: A prolonged siege occurred at the **Hong Kong Polytechnic University**.
- **November 24**: Pro-democracy candidates won a **landslide victory** in District Council elections.

## **Aftermath**
The protests significantly impacted Hong Kong's economy, tourism, and social fabric, and drew widespread international attention. The events of 2019 remain a pivotal and deeply consequential chapter in Hong Kong's modern history.
