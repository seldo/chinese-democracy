## Hong Kong as an International Financial Centre: Change Since 2020

Hong Kong's standing as an international financial centre has followed a clear "decline then rebound" trajectory since 2020, though the centre's underlying character has shifted toward mainland China.

**Rankings: a fall and recovery.** In the Z/Yen Global Financial Centres Index, Hong Kong slipped from third to sixth place in March 2020 ([GFCI 27](https://www.longfinance.net/programmes/distributed-futures/publications/global-financial-centres-index-27/)), and Singapore briefly overtook it in 2022. By 2025, however, Hong Kong had reclaimed third place globally, ahead of Singapore ([GFCI 37, March 2025](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202503/20/P2025032000391.htm); [GFCI 39](https://www.zyen.com/publications/public-reports/the-global-financial-centres-index-39/)).

**Equities and IPOs.** The Hang Seng Index fell for four consecutive years from 2020 (losses of 3–15% annually), and India's market capitalisation overtook Hong Kong's for the first time in 2024 ([ThinkChina](https://www.thinkchina.sg/economy/hong-kong-makes-comeback-international-financial-centre)). IPO fundraising collapsed from a near-record HK$400bn in 2020 (second globally) to sixth place in 2023, behind India and Indonesia. A 2025 rebound was dramatic: CATL's HK$41bn listing helped Hong Kong reclaim the top global IPO spot, with 2025 proceeds up 231% year-on-year to US$37.4bn ([HKEX](https://www.hkexgroup.com/Media-Centre/Insight/Insight/2026/HKEX-Insight/ECM-performance-in-2025?sc_lang=en)).

**Drivers of the downturn.** Three forces were widely cited: the 2020 National Security Law and associated political uncertainty; US–China rivalry (Washington ended Hong Kong's special customs treatment in July 2020 and restricted investment); and the mainland's economic slowdown and property-sector defaults. The number of foreign companies' regional headquarters fell for years to an 11-year low in 2023, though it rose again in 2024. US foreign direct investment stock declined around US$1bn from 2020, but then rose 15.3% to US$53bn in 2023 ([US State Dept 2025](https://www.state.gov/reports/2025-investment-climate-statements/hong-kong)).

**A structural shift.** Observers note Hong Kong is becoming "a financial centre more focused on China." Mainland companies now account for roughly 70% of Hang Seng Index weighting (up 10 percentage points from 2021), and mainland investors contribute about 45% of daily turnover ([ThinkChina](https://www.thinkchina.sg/economy/hong-kong-makes-comeback-international-financial-centre)). Economist Stephen Roach, who declared "Hong Kong is over" in 2024, publicly retracted that view in 2025.

**Assessment.** Hong Kong retains its top-three global ranking and has recovered IPO and equity momentum, but its resilience now rests heavily on Beijing's policy support and mainland capital rather than the broad Western investor base that once defined it. The centre's status appears stabilised but structurally transformed.
