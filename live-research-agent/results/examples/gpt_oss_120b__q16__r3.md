## Briefing

**Briefing: How Hong Kong’s Status as an International Financial Centre Has Evolved Since 2020**

**1. Overview of the shift**

Since 2020 Hong Kong has moved from a period of contraction and reputational risk to a modest rebound, but its profile has been reshaped. The city remains one of the world’s top three financial hubs, yet the composition of its markets, the sources of capital, and the perception of risk have changed dramatically.

**2. Rankings and market performance**

* **Global Financial Centres Index (GFCI).** In the GFCI‑38 (2023) Hong Kong ranked **3rd globally** with an overall score of 764, a slight dip from its 2nd‑place peak in 2019. By the GFCI‑39 release (September 2025) the city’s rating rose to **765**, keeping the **third‑place** position and regaining the top spot in “FinTech” and “business environment” categories【source】(https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202509/25/P2025092500970.htm). This shows that, while still elite, Hong Kong’s relative standing has been challenged by Singapore, New York and London, and by the rise of mainland Chinese centres such as Shanghai and Shenzhen.

* **IPO activity.** Hong Kong’s share of global IPO proceeds fell sharply after 2020, slipping to **6th place** in 2023 behind India, Indonesia and the United States【source】(https://www.thinkchina.sg/economy/hong-kong-makes-comeback-international-financial-centre). The Hang Seng Index posted **four consecutive years of decline** (‑3 % to ‑15 % annually) from 2020‑2023, and India’s market capitalisation overtook Hong Kong’s for the first time in 2023. A resurgence began in 2024‑2025, highlighted by CATL’s HK$41 bn listing, which propelled Hong Kong back to the **top spot in global IPO rankings for H1 2025**【source】(https://www.thinkchina.sg/economy/hong-kong-makes-comeback-international-financial-centre).

* **Asset & wealth management.** The HKMA reports that assets under management (AUM) reached **US$35.1 trn** at the end of 2024, a **13 % year‑on‑year increase**, with net fund inflows surging **81 %** to US$705 bn【source】(https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202509/25/P2025092500970.htm). This reflects renewed confidence from both mainland and overseas investors, even as the market’s composition has become more China‑centric.

**3. Drivers of change**

| Factor | Impact (2020‑2025) |
|--------|-------------------|
| **National security law (June 2020)** | Created perception of reduced political freedom, prompting some multinational headquarters to relocate and prompting the U.S. to end Hong Kong’s special customs status in 2020. The effect was more pronounced on reputation than on direct financial flows, but it contributed to a dip in foreign‑direct investment and a decline in regional HQ numbers (an 11‑year low in 2023)【source】(https://www.thinkchina.sg/economy/hong-kong-makes-comeback-international-financial-centre). |
| **COVID‑19 “zero‑COVID” policy** | Prolonged border closures (2020‑2022) forced expatriates and businesses to move to more open Asian hubs, accelerating the outflow of talent and reducing market liquidity. Analysts attribute the sharper decline in market turnover during 2021‑2022 to these restrictions rather than to politics【source】(https://www.thinkchina.sg/economy/hong-kong-makes-comeback-international-financial-centre). |
| **U.S.–China strategic rivalry** | The U.S. stripped Hong Kong of preferential treatment in 2020, and later limited investments in Chinese tech and semiconductor sectors, curbing foreign participation in the HKEX. Nonetheless, the rivalry also opened “China‑focused” capital‑raising opportunities, as mainland firms seek a global gateway that is not subject to U.S. sanctions. |
| **Mainland policy shifts** | Recent mainland incentives (e.g., relaxed listing rules, the “dual‑track” system, and the “stock connect” expansion) have drawn Chinese firms to list in Hong Kong, boosting IPO volumes and AUM. At the same time, volatility in mainland real‑estate and tech crackdowns adds sector‑specific risk to the Hang Seng Index. |
| **FinTech and digital‑asset strategy** | Hong Kong’s fintech ecosystem leapt to **world‑leading** status in the 2025 GFCI, backed by regulatory sandboxes, a stablecoin regime, and a digital‑asset licensing framework announced in 2024. This has attracted global fintech firms and helped diversify the financial services mix【source】(https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202509/25/P2025092500970.htm). |

**4. Structural evolution**

* **Greater “China‑focus”.** Mainland companies now account for **≈70 %** of the Hang Seng Index weighting (up 10 pp since 2021) and generate roughly **45 %** of daily trading volume (2025)【source】(https://www.thinkchina.sg/economy/hong-kong-makes-comeback-international-financial-centre). The city is increasingly viewed as a **gateway to China** rather than a neutral, “West‑East” hub.

* **Shift in talent pool.** Expatriate numbers fell during the pandemic, but the HKMA’s 2024 half‑yearly report notes a **moderate rebound** in professional services employment as the border re‑opened and new listings attracted talent from mainland financial centres.

* **Diversification into new asset classes.** Hong Kong is expanding into **gold trading, commodity futures, and offshore RMB bond issuance**, all highlighted in the 2025 Policy Address and the HKMA’s financial‑stability outlook. These initiatives aim to offset the declining share of traditional banking revenues.

**5. Outlook (2025‑2028)**

1. **Ranking stability** – The GFCI projects Hong Kong will likely stay in the **top‑three** for the next three years, provided political risk does not intensify and the city maintains its “super‑connector” role between mainland China and global capital.

2. **Capital flows** – Net inflows into Hong Kong’s banking system reached **US$44 bn** in 2025, a **≈300 % YoY increase**【source】(https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202509/25/P2025092500970.htm). Continued mainland‑driven inflows are expected, though they will be sensitive to Beijing’s macro‑policy and to any further U.S. sanctions.

3. **Sectoral risk** – Heavy weighting toward mainland‑controlled sectors (real estate, state‑linked tech) makes the Hang Seng Index vulnerable to policy swings. Diversification into fintech, green finance, and offshore RMB markets is a strategic priority for the Hong Kong Monetary Authority and the Financial Services Development Council.

4. **Regulatory environment** – The city’s “one‑country‑two‑systems” framework remains a unique selling point, but the perception of autonomy is under continuous scrutiny. Ongoing dialogue with international regulators (e.g., IOSCO, FATF) will be essential to preserve cross‑border market access.

**6. Key take‑aways**

* Hong Kong remains a **top‑three global financial centre**, but its **comparative advantage has shifted** from a broadly international hub to a **China‑oriented gateway** with strong fintech and digital‑asset capabilities.
* **Political and pandemic shocks (2020‑2022)** caused a measurable decline in IPO activity, market capitalisation, and foreign headquarters, yet **policy adjustments (post‑2023)** and mainland incentives have driven a **partial recovery**.
* The **future trajectory** hinges on the balance between **geopolitical risk** (U.S.–China tensions, sanctions) and **strategic diversification** (fintech, green finance, commodity markets). Maintaining high‑quality regulatory standards and preserving a perception of legal independence will be critical for sustaining Hong Kong’s status as an international financial centre.
