## Briefing

**Briefing: Labor Conditions in Xinjiang's Cotton Industry**

Xinjiang produces roughly 80–90% of China's cotton and about a fifth of global supply, making its labor practices a major international concern. Since 2019–2020, researchers, journalists, and governments have alleged that Uyghurs and other Turkic minorities have been subjected to state-coerced labor in cotton picking and processing, primarily through "labor transfer" programs rather than direct camp-linked work.

The most detailed evidence comes from analysis of Chinese government documents, state media, and satellite imagery. Researcher Adrian Zenz estimated that labor transfers mobilized over 500,000 ethnic minority workers into cotton picking, arguing that coercive elements—quotas, political indoctrination, restricted ability to refuse—render consent meaningless (https://newlinesinstitute.org/intl-law-peace/coercive-labor-in-xinjiang-labor-transfer-and-the-mobilization-of-ethnic-minorities-to-pick-cotton/; https://www.theguardian.com/world/2020/dec/15/xinjiang-china-more-than-half-a-million-forced-to-pick-cotton-report-finds). A peer-reviewed study compared the system to Uzbekistan's former state-mobilized cotton harvest (https://online.ucpress.edu/cpcs/article/56/2/1/196124/Coercive-Labor-in-the-Cotton-Harvest-in-the). The Australian Strategic Policy Institute documented a broader "Xinjiang Aid" labor transfer scheme sending Uyghurs to factories across China (https://www.aspi.org.au/report/uyghurs-sale/). The US Department of Labor describes these programs as institutionalized forced labor (https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ilab/against-their-will-the-situation-in-xinjiang), and the UN human rights office's 2022 assessment found evidence of forced labor schemes, though China rallied diplomatic opposition to the report (https://www.reuters.com/world/china/china-rallies-support-over-xinjiang-report-un-rights-meeting-2022-09-13/).

Governments and industry have responded. US Customs issued a region-wide ban on Xinjiang cotton in January 2021 (https://natlawreview.com/article/united-states-bans-cotton-and-tomato-products-xinjiang-citing-human-rights), later superseded by the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (effective June 2022), which presumes all Xinjiang goods involve forced labor unless proven otherwise. The Better Cotton Initiative ceased field activities in Xinjiang in 2020, citing an "untenable operating environment" (https://www.hrw.org/news/2020/10/25/chinas-untenable-operating-environment-business-xinjiang), though it later removed its statement amid Chinese pressure (https://www.axios.com/2021/04/13/xinjiang-cotton-watchdog). Sheffield Hallam University's "Laundering Cotton" report traced Xinjiang cotton into dozens of global brands' supply chains (https://www.shu.ac.uk/helena-kennedy-centre-international-justice/research-and-projects/all-projects/forced-labour-reports), and a 2025 investigation linked sanctioned cotton to animal-feed supply chains (https://www.thebureauinvestigates.com/stories/2025-05-30/the-world-sanctioned-xinjiang-cotton.-china-turned-it-into-chicken-feed).

China categorically denies forced labor. Its 2020 white paper frames labor transfers as voluntary poverty-alleviation employment with legal protections (https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/whitepaper/202009/17/content_WS5f62cef6c6d0f7257693c192.html), and officials note mechanization reached roughly 70% of harvesting by 2020, reducing demand for manual pickers (https://ca.china-embassy.gov.cn/eng/zt/xingjiang2021/202104/t20210401_9029489.htm).

Key uncertainties remain: independent audits are effectively impossible in Xinjiang, so current scale and conditions are hard to verify; mechanization has genuinely reduced hand-picking, though researchers argue coercive transfers continued into 2021–2022; and critics of the forced-labor findings note reliance on researchers like Zenz, whose interpretations Beijing disputes. The core dispute is less whether mass labor mobilization occurred—Chinese documents confirm it did—than whether participation was meaningfully voluntary.
