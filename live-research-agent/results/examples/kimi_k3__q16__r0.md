## Briefing

Hong Kong’s status has changed less by collapse than by rotation: it lost some breadth and perceived autonomy during 2020–23, then regained momentum in 2024–25 as a China-connected fundraising and wealth hub. In the Global Financial Centres Index, Hong Kong fell behind Singapore in September 2022, when New York and London led and Singapore took third while Hong Kong came fourth (https://www.longfinance.net/publications/long-finance-reports/the-global-financial-centres-index-32/; https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202209/22/P2022092200660.htm). It recovered to third in GFCI 36 in September 2024 (https://www.longfinance.net/programmes/financial-centre-futures/global-financial-centres-index/press/press-release-global-financial-centres-index-36-leading-centres-remain-strong-during-geo-political-challenges/), retained third ahead of Singapore in March 2025 (https://www.longfinance.net/publications/long-finance-reports/the-global-financial-centres-index-37/), and in March 2026 still ranked third globally and first in Asia-Pacific with a rating of 765 (https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202603/26/P2026032600501.htm).

The equity cycle tells the same story. After the pandemic/security-law shock and China’s property and regulatory downturn, IPO activity was weak in 2022–23; by 2025 HKEX says Hong Kong was the world’s top IPO fundraising venue, with proceeds up 231% year on year to US$37.4bn (https://www.hkexgroup.com/Media-Centre/Insight/Insight/2026/HKEX-Insight/ECM-performance-in-2025?sc_lang=en). The rebound is heavily Mainland-linked, reinforcing Hong Kong’s “super-connector” role; the IMF similarly describes it as a global financial center and connector to the Mainland (https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/002/2026/114/article-A001-en.pdf).

Policy has leaned into that niche. ETFs were added to Stock Connect in July 2022 (https://www.hkex.online/en/news/news-release/2022/2207042news); in 2023 Hong Kong launched the HKD/RMB dual-counter model and said international companies listed in Hong Kong could join Stock Connect (https://www.hkexgroup.com/Media-Centre/Insight/Insight/2023/Nicolas-Aguzin/Unrivalled-Connectivity-Unlimited-Possibilities?sc_lang=en; https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202304/25/P2023042500555.htm). It also courted private wealth: the New Capital Investment Entrant Scheme began taking applications on 1 March 2024 (https://www.mondaq.com/hongkong/real-estate/1443900/hong-kongs-new-capital-investment-entrant-scheme), alongside family-office tax concessions for qualifying vehicles (https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202506/25/P2025062500584p.htm). InvestHK-linked reporting put overseas/Mainland-parent companies in Hong Kong at a record 9,960 in 2024 (https://www.linkedin.com/posts/investhk-london_record-high-numbers-of-companies-and-start-ups-activity-7275122509346140161-F6az).

The offset is that the centre is more exposed to China’s cycle and to doubts about predictability after the security-law era and strict zero-COVID controls; even local commentary in March 2023 said the city was healing but faced many challenges (https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/201573/Hong-Kong-is-healing-but-many-challenges-remain). Net: still a top-three global centre, but less universally “international” and more explicitly the offshore capital-markets gateway to China.
