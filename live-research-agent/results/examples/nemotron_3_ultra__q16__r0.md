## Briefing

Since 2020, Hong Kong’s standing as an international financial centre (IFC) has followed a distinct U-shaped trajectory: a sharp pandemic-era dip followed by a steady recovery to its pre-2020 perch in the global top three.

**2020–2022: The Pandemic Dip and Loss of Regional Lead**  
In early 2020 Hong Kong still ranked third globally behind New York and London in the Global Financial Centres Index (GFCI)【https://www.cato.org/cato-journal/winter-2021/financial-development-hong-kong-china-hayekian-perspective】. However, the city’s adherence to China’s zero-Covid strategy—mandatory hotel quarantine, closed mainland border, and stringent social-distancing rules—triggered a talent exodus and deterred international business travel. By September 2022 (GFCI 32) Singapore overtook Hong Kong to become Asia’s top financial hub, pushing Hong Kong to fourth place globally【https://www.forbesindia.com/article/news/global-financial-centres-index-(gfci)-rankings-hong-kong-replaced-by-singapore-as-asias-top-finance-centre/80045/1】. The Hang Seng Index recorded annual declines for four consecutive years from 2020, reflecting weakened sentiment【https://www.thinkchina.sg/economy/hong-kong-makes-comeback-international-financial-centre】.

**2023–2024: Reopening and Ranking Recovery**  
The lifting of all pandemic restrictions in early 2023 marked a turning point. Hong Kong reclaimed third place globally in GFCI 36 (September 2024) with a rating of 760, just two points behind London and one behind New York【https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/220819/Hong-Kong-rises-to-third-place-in-Global-Financial-Centres-Index】. The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) highlighted the city’s deep liquidity pool, renminbi business, and fintech leadership as structural strengths that survived the downturn【https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/key-functions/international-financial-centre/hong-kong-as-an-international-financial-centre/】.

**2025–Present: Consolidation at Global Number Three**  
In the latest GFCI 38 (September 2025) and GFCI 39 (March 2026), Hong Kong holds firm at third globally with ratings of 764 and 765 respectively, maintaining a narrow gap to London and New York【https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202509/25/P2025092500970.htm】【https://www.fsdc.org.hk/en/media/fsdc-welcomes-global-financial-centres-index-39-hong-kong-solidifies-global-top-three-position-and-fintech-leadership】. The Financial Services Development Council notes Hong Kong now ranks first worldwide in fintech and leads in insurance and capital-markets competitiveness【https://www.fsdc.org.hk/en/media/fsdc-welcomes-global-financial-centres-index-38-s-reaffirmation-of-hong-kong-s-top-three-position】.

**Structural Shifts Beneath the Rankings**  
While the headline ranking has rebounded, the competitive landscape has shifted. Singapore’s rise reflects its success as a wealth-management and family-office hub, attracting assets and talent that left Hong Kong during the pandemic. Conversely, Hong Kong has deepened integration with the Greater Bay Area and mainland capital markets, leveraging Stock Connect, Bond Connect, and the new Swap Connect to cement its role as China’s primary offshore financing window. The National Security Law (2020) and subsequent judicial interpretations remain a concern for some foreign firms, but the government’s 2023–24 policy address emphasised regulatory reforms, tax concessions for family offices, and a HK$30 billion Co-Investment Fund to recapture deal flow.

**Outlook**  
Hong Kong’s IFC status has proven resilient: it absorbed a severe exogenous shock, lost its regional crown temporarily, and regained its global top-three position within three years. The next test is whether it can narrow the rating gap with London and New York while fending off Singapore’s challenge for Asian pre-eminence—a contest now fought on fintech innovation, green finance, and talent retention rather than pandemic policy alone.
