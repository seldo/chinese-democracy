## Briefing

Hong Kong’s status as an international financial centre (IFC) has followed a sharp V‑shaped trajectory since 2020: a sudden reputational dip during the pandemic and political unrest, followed by a sustained recovery that has restored its top‑three global ranking.

**The 2020 shock.** In the March 2020 Global Financial Centres Index (GFCI), Hong Kong fell three places to sixth globally, overtaken by Tokyo, Shanghai and Singapore. Anonymous respondents cited “social unrest” and expected “brain drain” as key drivers of the decline (SCMP, March 27 2020)【https://www.scmp.com/news/hong-kong/hong-kong-economy/article/3077299/hong-kong-drops-sixth-new-global-financial-centre】. The drop coincided with the onset of COVID‑19 travel restrictions and the imposition of the National Security Law, which raised foreign‑investor concerns about autonomy and talent retention.

**Steady climb back to third.** By the September 2022 GFCI, Singapore had briefly displaced Hong Kong as Asia’s top centre (Forbes India, Sept 2022)【https://www.forbesindia.com/article/news/global-financial-centres-index-(gfci)-rankings-hong-kong-replaced-by-singapore-as-asias-top-finance-centre/80045/1】, but Hong Kong reclaimed third place globally in the March 2025 edition (GFCI 37) with a rating of 760, and held it in September 2025 (GFCI 38, rating 764) and March 2026 (GFCI 39, rating 765) (HK Govt, Mar 2025)【https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202503/20/P2025032000391.htm】; (HK Govt, Mar 2026)【https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202603/26/P2026032600501.htm】. The government highlights that sub‑indices for human capital, infrastructure and financial‑sector development now rank second worldwide, while business environment and reputation rank third.

**Structural pillars of the rebound.** Three factors underpin the recovery. First, **mainland integration** has deepened: expanded Stock/Bond Connect schemes, the Cross‑boundary Wealth Management Connect in the Greater Bay Area, and new offshore RMB products (e.g., Northbound Bond Connect repos) reinforce Hong Kong’s role as China’s primary global liquidity window (HK Govt, Mar 2025)【https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202503/20/P2025032000391.htm】. Second, **sectoral leadership** persists: Hong Kong ranks first globally in investment management, insurance and finance, and third in banking (GFCI 37). Third, **fintech and green finance** are rising fast—fintech ranking jumped to fourth globally, and a 2024 ISSB‑aligned sustainability disclosure roadmap positions the city as an early adopter of global climate‑reporting standards.

**Lingering vulnerabilities.** Despite the ranking recovery, cyclical headwinds remain. The Hang Seng Index fell in each of the four years 2020‑2023, reflecting weak IPO pipelines and capital outflows (ThinkChina, Jul 2025)【https://www.thinkchina.sg/economy/hong-kong-makes-comeback-international-financial-centre】. Talent competition with Singapore—especially in wealth management and crypto—intensifies, and geopolitical friction (U.S.–China tensions, sanctions risk) continues to complicate the “super‑connector” narrative.

**Outlook.** Hong Kong has re‑established its top‑tier GFCI standing, but the margin over Singapore is narrow (often single‑digit rating points). Sustaining the position will depend on translating mainland‑driven volume into high‑value services, reversing the equity‑market slump, and convincing global talent that the city’s legal and lifestyle proposition remains distinct.
