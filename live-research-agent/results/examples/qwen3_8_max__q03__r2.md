## Briefing

**Briefing: How Hong Kong's National Security Law Has Affected Foreign Businesses**

Since Beijing imposed the National Security Law (NSL) in June 2020, and Hong Kong enacted the Article 23 Safeguarding National Security Ordinance (SNSO) in March 2024, foreign firms have faced a more uncertain legal environment. The U.S. State Department's Investment Climate Statement says the NSL "undermined Hong Kong's autonomy and introduced heightened uncertainty for foreign and local firms," warning that the SNSO's "extremely broad and vague definitions of 'espionage,' 'state secret,' 'external interference,' and potential extraterritorial application could affect or impair routine business activities" (https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/investment-climate-statement-hong-kong-macau).

The clearest business impact has been on compliance and risk management. Article 23's expanded state-secrets provisions have raised concerns for due diligence, consulting, auditing, and financial analysis, prompting firms to add contractual safeguards and ongoing risk monitoring (https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2025-september/evolving-regulatory-relationship/). In September 2024, Washington updated a multi-agency advisory flagging data-privacy, transparency, and sanctions-exposure risks (https://2021-2025.state.gov/hong-kong-business-advisory/).

Sentiment has shifted over time. Early AmCham Hong Kong surveys showed alarm: in 2021, 42% of members surveyed planned or considered relocating, with discomfort over the NSL a leading reason (https://www.cnbc.com/2021/05/12/hong-kong-security-law-amcham-finds-42percent-of-members-surveyed-plan-to-leave.html). AmCham's 2022 survey found over eight in ten businesses had been affected by the NSL, with staff morale and emigration-related attrition as key issues (https://www.amcham.org.hk/sites/default/files/content-files/AmCham%20HK%202022%20Business%20Sentiment%20Study%20Report%20(English).pdf). By 2026, however, 74% of AmCham respondents reported no negative operational impact, confidence in the rule of law rose to 94%, and 92% of multinationals said they had no plans to relocate headquarters (https://www.amcham.org.hk/news/amcham-hks-2026-business-sentiment-survey-finds-improved-outlook-2026-amid-ongoing-global).

Official statistics support a resilience narrative. Companies with mainland or overseas parent companies hit a record 9,960 in 2024 (https://www.chinadailyhk.com/hk/article/600547) and a record 11,070 in 2025 (https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202603/18/P2026031800288p.htm), while registered companies reached an all-time high of 1.56 million by end-2025.

Yet structural shifts point to caution. The number of foreign multinationals using Hong Kong as a regional base has declined steadily since 2020, with regional headquarters falling by 75 to 1,336 in 2023 (https://www.cedb.gov.hk/en/legco-business/questions/2024/pr27032024a.html; https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/investment-climate-statement-hong-kong-macau). U.S. firm counts have held roughly steady at about 1,390, but mainland Chinese firms now make up the largest share of "foreign" offices, more than doubling since 2013.

In sum, the NSL has not triggered a mass exodus, and company registrations remain strong, but it has raised compliance costs, chilled some regional-headquarters activity, and—combined with U.S. sanctions and differential-treatment measures—made legal risk a central factor in foreign firms' Hong Kong planning.
