## Briefing

**Hong Kong's Financial Centre Status Since 2020: Decline, Then Resurgence**

Hong Kong's position as an international financial centre has undergone a turbulent cycle since 2020 — first eroded by political and pandemic shocks, then substantially rebuilt through deeper integration with mainland China.

**2020–2022: Erosion.** The June 2020 National Security Law marked a turning point. Beijing's sweeping new powers altered the city's legal and political environment (https://www.cfr.org/backgrounders/hong-kong-freedoms-democracy-protests-china-crackdown), prompting multinational firms to reassess operations and relocate staff (https://www.cipe.org/resources/hong-kong-lost-its-appeal-as-a-financial-hub-for-asia-after-the-national-security-law/). Fitch downgraded Hong Kong to AA- in April 2020, citing weakening links with mainland China (https://www.fitchratings.com/research/sovereigns/fitch-downgrades-hong-kong-to-aa-from-aa-outlook-stable-20-04-2020). Strict pandemic quarantine rules and an expat exodus compounded the damage; the population fell and the financial sector faced a talent crunch (https://www.reuters.com/markets/europe/hong-kongs-financial-sector-faces-talent-crunch-expats-head-exit-2022-01-23/). Singapore overtook Hong Kong as Asia's top-ranked centre in the Global Financial Centres Index during this period.

**2023–2024: Stabilisation.** The Article 23 security legislation in March 2024 raised fresh concerns among Western businesses (https://www.nytimes.com/2024-03-19/business/hong-kong-article-23-china-economy.html), and analysts documented ongoing risks to the commercial environment (https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/in-depth-research-reports/report/fractured-foundations-assessing-risks-to-hong-kongs-business-environment/). Yet by September 2024, Hong Kong regained its lead over Singapore in the GFCI (https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-09-25/hong-kong-regains-lead-over-singapore-as-finance-center-survey-says).

**2025–2026: Resurgence driven by mainland flows.** Hong Kong reclaimed the crown as the world's top IPO venue, with listings surging eightfold in the first half of 2025 to $13.5 billion raised (https://www.law.com/international-edition/2025-07-22/the-ipo-crown-has-returned-to-hong-kong-but-how-will-global-firms-compete-with-the-new-cowboys-in-town-/). Southbound Stock Connect turnover hit a record HK$121.1 billion daily average in 2025, more than double 2024 (https://www.hkexgroup.com/Media-Centre/Insight/Insight/2026/HKEX-Insight/Stock-Connect-2025-Review?sc_lang=en). The number of companies with mainland or overseas parents reached a record 11,070, up 11% (https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202601/26/P2026012600135.htm), and real GDP surpassed its pre-pandemic peak (https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/002/2026/114/article-A001-en.xml). In GFCI 39 (March 2026), Hong Kong ranked third globally with a rating of 765, first in Asia-Pacific and top for fintech (https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202603/26/P2026032600501.htm; https://www.longfinance.net/programmes/financial-centre-futures/global-financial-centres-index/gfci-publications/the-global-financial-centres-index-39/).

**Assessment.** Hong Kong has retained — and by some metrics strengthened — its global ranking, but its character has changed. The centre is now more dependent on mainland capital flows and Chinese issuers, and less on Western intermediation. Its status endures, but as a bridge for China rather than a freewheeling Western-style hub.
