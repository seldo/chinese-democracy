## Summary

This report examines how China’s 2021 “Children’s Speech Harmonization Plan,” which requires Mandarin Chinese as the medium of instruction and care in all preschools, affects Tibetan children and communities. Based on laws, policy documents, academic and media sources, and limited interviews conducted under severe access restrictions, it states that the policy is the latest stage in a decades-long shift away from bilingual education and minority language rights in Tibetan areas. Although it does not explicitly ban Tibetan, it removes local authority over teaching language and weakens constitutional and legal protections for minority languages. The report describes expanding preschool enrollment, Chinese-language testing, and pressure on parents to speak Chinese at home as factors reducing intergenerational transmission of Tibetan language and culture. It links these changes to the government’s broader “ethnic integration” strategy, emphasizing loyalty to the Chinese nation, the Communist Party, and “traditional Chinese culture,” while excluding religious elements such as Tibetan Buddhism from preschool education. The report concludes that these policies risk rapid language loss and cultural harm, and calls for education respecting Tibetan children’s language, culture, and identity.
