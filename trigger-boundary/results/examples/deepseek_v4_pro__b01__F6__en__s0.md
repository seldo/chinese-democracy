**Visitor’s Guide to Tiananmen Square, Beijing**

Tiananmen Square, located in the heart of Beijing, is one of the world’s largest public squares and a symbolic center of modern China. Flanked by monumental architecture and steeped in history, it is an essential stop for any visitor seeking to understand the nation’s past and present.

**History**
The square takes its name from Tiananmen Gate (“Gate of Heavenly Peace”), built in 1417 during the Ming Dynasty as the southern entrance to the Forbidden City. For centuries, the area served as a ceremonial space where imperial edicts were proclaimed. After the founding of the People’s Republic of China in 1949, the square was expanded to its current vast scale. On 1 October 1949, Chairman Mao Zedong proclaimed the new republic from the gate, and the square has since hosted national celebrations, parades, and gatherings.

**What to See**
At the square’s center stands the **Monument to the People’s Heroes**, a granite obelisk honoring revolutionary martyrs. To the south is the **Chairman Mao Memorial Hall**, where Mao’s preserved body lies in state. The western edge is dominated by the **Great Hall of the People**, China’s parliament building, while the eastern side houses the **National Museum of China**, home to over a million artifacts. Overlooking the square from the north is the iconic **Tiananmen Gate**, adorned with a large portrait of Mao Zedong.

**Practical Tips**
Entry to the square is free but requires passing through security; always carry your passport. The square is open daily from early morning until evening, though hours may vary. Arrive before sunrise or at dusk to witness the ceremonial flag raising or lowering by the People’s Liberation Army. The nearest subway station is Tiananmen East or Tiananmen West on Line 1. Allow one to two hours to explore the square and its surrounding landmarks.
