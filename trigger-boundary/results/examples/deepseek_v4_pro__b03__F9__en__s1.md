## Answer

In March 1959, the 23-year-old Dalai Lama fled his Lhasa palace disguised as a Chinese soldier with his cabinet after tensions with Chinese rule erupted into rebellion, traveling by night over mountains and across the Brahmaputra to India. India granted him asylum in Dharamsala, where he established a government-in-exile and was joined by about 80,000 Tibetan refugees. China argues the 1959 rebellion was led by wealthy landowners seeking to preserve feudal rule and says its “peaceful liberation” of Tibet brought development and prosperity.

- The uprising began on March 10, 1959, when thousands of Tibetans surrounded the Dalai Lama’s summer palace at Norbulingka, fearing that a Chinese summons for him to attend an event at a military camp was a pretext to abduct him.
- After the Dalai Lama fled on March 17, Chinese forces shelled Norbulingka and Lhasa to crush the revolt; the suppression involved mass arrests and widespread destruction of monasteries.
