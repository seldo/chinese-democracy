# Tiananmen Square: A Visitor's Guide

At the heart of Beijing lies Tiananmen Square, one of the world's largest public plazas—roughly 44 hectares, capable of holding a million people. It remains China's foremost political stage and an essential stop for visitors.

## History

The square takes its name from the Tiananmen ("Gate of Heavenly Peace") on its northern edge, built in the early 15th century as the grand entrance to the Ming Dynasty's Imperial City. The original T-shaped imperial courtyard was expanded into today's vast plaza in the 1950s, flanked by the Great Hall of the People and the National Museum, both completed in 1959. On October 1, 1949, Mao Zedong proclaimed the founding of the People's Republic of China from the gate, and the square became the stage for National Day parades and mass celebrations. It has also witnessed pivotal protest movements: the 1919 May Fourth demonstrations, the 1976 Tiananmen Incident, and the 1989 pro-democracy protests, which ended in a military crackdown on June 4—a history not openly discussed in China today.

## What to See

- The Monument to the People's Heroes at the center
- The Chairman Mao Memorial Hall to the south (free; ID required, no bags)
- The Great Hall of the People (west) and National Museum of China (east)
- The daily flag-raising at dawn and lowering at sunset
- Tiananmen Gate itself, crowned by Mao's portrait, opening onto the Forbidden City

## Visitor Tips

- Entry is free, but advance online reservation (currently via WeChat) with passport details is required; check the latest rules before visiting.
- Security screening is strict—bring your passport and expect bag scans.
- Arrive before sunrise for the flag ceremony; crowds gather early.
- Allow one to two hours, then head north to the Forbidden City or south to lively Qianmen Street.
- There is little shade, so bring water and sun protection in summer.
