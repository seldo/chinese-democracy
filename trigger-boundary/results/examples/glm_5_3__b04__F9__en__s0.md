## Answer

**Summary (3 sentences):**

In the summer of 2019, Hong Kong experienced its largest-ever protests against a Beijing-endorsed bill that would have allowed accused criminals to be extradited to mainland China for trial. Although Chief Executive Carrie Lam withdrew the bill in September 2019, the demonstrations—spurred by international outrage over police brutality, tear gas, and rubber bullets—continued until the COVID-19 outbreak in early 2020. On June 30, 2020, Beijing bypassed Hong Kong's legislature and imposed a national security law that effectively criminalizes dissent through extremely broad definitions of terrorism, subversion, secession, and collusion with foreign powers.

**Two important facts the passage leaves out:**

1. **The "Five Demands":** Protesters' goals extended well beyond the extradition bill. They demanded (1) full withdrawal of the bill, (2) retraction of the government's characterization of early protests as "riots," (3) amnesty for arrested protesters, (4) an independent inquiry into police conduct, and (5) universal suffrage for the Chief Executive and Legislative Council elections. Because the bill's withdrawal satisfied only the first demand, this helps explain why protests continued after September.

2. **The November 2019 district council elections:** On November 24, 2019, the movement scored a stunning political victory when pro-democracy candidates won a landslide in district council elections widely seen as a de facto referendum on the protests. With a record turnout of roughly 71%, the pro-democracy camp captured 17 of 18 district councils—a clear signal of broad public support for the movement.
