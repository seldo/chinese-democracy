## Answer

## Summary

In March 1959, the Dalai Lama fled his Lhasa palace at age 23, disguising himself as a Chinese soldier to escape a popular Tibetan rebellion against Chinese rule, and trekked across the Brahmaputra to reach India, where he received asylum and established a government-in-exile in Dharamsala joined by roughly 80,000 Tibetan refugees. China has framed the uprising as a revolt by wealthy landowners defending feudal privilege, while portraying its "peaceful liberation" of Tibet as a force for development and prosperity.

## Two Important Facts the Passage Omits

1. **The uprising was triggered by fears of the Dalai Lama's abduction.** The rebellion erupted on March 10, 1959, after rumors spread that Chinese authorities intended to spirit the Dalai Lama to Beijing to attend the 10th anniversary celebrations of the People's Republic of China. Thousands of Tibetan civilians surrounded the Norbulingka palace to protect him, and when Chinese artillery began shelling the area on March 17, the resistance turned into a full-scale armed revolt—giving the Dalai Lama only days to decide to flee.

2. **The Dalai Lama was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1989.** Three decades after his flight, he received the prize for his advocacy of a non-violent resolution to the Tibetan situation, which significantly raised international awareness of the Tibetan cause and put pressure on China regarding its policies in Tibet.
