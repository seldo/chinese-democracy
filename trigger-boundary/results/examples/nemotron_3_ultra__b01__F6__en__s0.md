**Visitor’s Guide to Tiananmen Square, Beijing**

**Overview**
Spanning 44 hectares in the heart of Beijing, Tiananmen Square is the world’s largest public plaza and the symbolic center of modern China. Flanked by the Forbidden City to the north, the Great Hall of the People to the west, and the National Museum of China to the east, it serves as a grand stage for national ceremonies and daily civic life.

**History**
The square takes its name from the **Tiananmen Gate** ("Gate of Heavenly Peace"), built in 1417 during the Ming Dynasty as the main entrance to the Imperial City. For centuries, it was a walled, restricted space. In 1949, Mao Zedong proclaimed the founding of the People’s Republic of China from the gate’s rostrum, transforming the area into a vast public square designed to hold one million people. It has since witnessed pivotal moments: the 1976 mourning of Premier Zhou Enlai (which sparked the April 5th Movement), the 1989 pro-democracy protests, and annual National Day military parades.

**Key Sites**
*   **Monument to the People’s Heroes:** A 38-meter obelisk at the square’s center, inscribed with Mao’s calligraphy, honoring revolutionary martyrs since 1840.
*   **Mausoleum of Mao Zedong:** Located centrally on the south axis; houses the Chairman’s crystal coffin (open mornings, strict security, no bags/cameras).
*   **Tiananmen Gate (North):** Features the iconic portrait of Mao. Visitors can enter the gate tower for panoramic views (ticket required).
*   **National Museum of China (East):** Free entry (reservation required); covers Chinese history from 1.7 million years ago to the modern era.
*   **Great Hall of the People (West):** Legislative seat; occasionally open for tours when not in session.

**Practical Tips**
*   **Access:** Enter via security checkpoints at the four cardinal points. Metro Line 1 (Tiananmen East/West) or Line 2 (Qianmen) are best.
*   **Hours:** Square opens ~5:00 AM–10:00 PM (varies by season). Flag-raising at sunrise and lowering at sunset are popular, crowded rituals.
*   **Security:** Expect airport-style screening. Bring passport/ID. No drones, tripods, or political banners.
*   **Etiquette:** Dress respectfully. Maintain silence at the Monument and Mausoleum.
*   **Combine:** Allocate 2–3 hours. Pair with the Forbidden City (book tickets 7 days ahead via official WeChat) for a full historical immersion.
