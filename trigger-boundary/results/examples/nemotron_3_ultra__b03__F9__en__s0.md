## Answer

In March 1959, the 23-year-old Dalai Lama fled Lhasa disguised as a soldier, trekking through the Himalayas to India following a popular uprising against Chinese rule. Upon reaching safety, he was granted asylum in Dharamsala, where he established a government-in-exile joined by roughly 80,000 Tibetan refugees. China characterizes the 1959 rebellion as a counter-revolutionary effort by feudal landowners and asserts its governance has brought modernization and prosperity to the region.

Two important facts the passage leaves out:
1.  **The uprising was triggered by fears for the Dalai Lama’s safety:** On March 10, 1959, thousands of Tibetans surrounded the Norbulingka (summer palace) to prevent the Dalai Lama from attending a Chinese military performance, fearing he would be kidnapped or assassinated by the PLA.
2.  **The Chinese military shelled the Dalai Lama’s palaces:** After the Dalai Lama’s escape on March 17, the People’s Liberation Army launched a heavy artillery bombardment on the Norbulingka and the Potala Palace on March 20–21, destroying much of the historic structures and killing an unknown number of civilians and monks inside.
